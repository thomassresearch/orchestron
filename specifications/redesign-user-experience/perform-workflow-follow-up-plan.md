# Perform workflow follow-up implementation plan

This plan covers the seven musician and producer findings against the released Perform workspace at `d44d4ea`. The target is a session in which a player can hear a variation, prepare the next pad, and adjust its sound while the arrangement keeps its place. Arrange stays above its editor, the Inspector stays on the right, and the browser document stays within the viewport.

Stage 1 makes an explicitly opened keyboard a shared playing surface within Perform. Editor selection, detail panels, Inspector state and scroll positions belong to each view. Stages 2–4 remain planned. [Current interaction rules](layout-and-interaction-spec.md#selection-and-panel-interactions) · [Original command inventory](baseline/command-map.md) · [Release measurements](milestone-7-release.md)

## Layout contract for every stage

Measure the actual room left after the application header, workspace commands, transport and status bar, including wrapped translations and errors. Give the remaining grid rows and columns shrinkable minima. The timeline, launcher, strip bank, Inspector, detail body and keyboard settings own their individual overflow.
- While open, keys occupy a bounded row above the status bar. The existing divider continues to resize the overview and detail within the remaining space. Closing the keys or detail immediately returns its space. Keep the preferred split height across temporary clamps caused by a smaller window or an open keyboard.
- Use the existing measured transition to one working pane when the overview and detail cannot both be used. In that mode show the chosen overview **or** editor above a compact playable octave with a range control. Keep the editor/overview return action, keyboard close action and transport reachable. In Live, keep the compact device picker and launch pads reachable alongside the keys. Longer settings and device lists scroll inside their own panels. On very short screens, move secondary commands into the existing command area’s overflow before reducing the keys or the active editing area to unusable targets.
- Opening a drawer, focusing a step or dropping a phrase may scroll its local container. Keep the document, timeline ruler and unrelated panels stationary. Use visible focus, named controls, keyboard alternatives to drag, and touch targets large enough to distinguish a pad launch from its preview. ### 1. Arrange — play against the clip draft

**Interaction.** With a pattern open and **Play edit** active, pressing **Keyboard** reveals the keys while the same occurrence keeps sounding. Keep the selected pass, linked/local scope, dirty values and editor scroll. On a short screen the player can switch the main pane between timeline and editor while the keys stay available. Identify the sounding occurrence and keep **Stop edit** reachable whenever its editor controls are out of sight.

**Implementation.** Separate keyboard visibility from the per-view `dock` field. Render the keyboard at a stable position beneath the working area, inside the performance-generation lifecycle and outside the subtree replaced on a view change. Reuse the existing piano-roll controls, MIDI callbacks, scale following and editable keyboard settings. Existing performances with several keyboards must retain access to each roll by its stable ID.

Opening or closing the keys must leave `clipEditingKey` and the active audition gesture alone. Keep explicit editor close, a change of audition target, Save, Stop and departure from Arrange on their existing clip-audition paths. Closing only the keyboard releases the notes played on it. Route **Stop edit** through the current audition owner and guard late acknowledgements by gesture and draft identity. **Pass when** a local variation on the second chorus pass keeps sounding through Keyboard open, chord down/up, divider resize and Keyboard close. Opening the keys emits no audition end or restart, draft save or transport seek. **Stop edit** restores the prior source once, and the original scope and unsaved variation are still available. ### 6. Mix — hold a chord through a sound adjustment

**Interaction.** Expose the same **Keyboard** toggle in Mix. An open keyboard travels with the player between Perform views until explicitly closed. The playing keyboard keeps its destination, so selecting an insert cannot redirect a held chord.

**Implementation.** Keep the actual key DOM nodes and their note owner mounted across navigation, expansion and responsive layout changes. Use the stable host from finding 1 throughout because moving a React portal to another host can unmount a captured touch target. Keep pointer IDs, keyboard key-up handling, overlapping Autochord note counts and original output channels with the keyboard instance. Keep held-note state out of saved performances and restored view state. Suspend hidden settings and meter work independently of the sounding keyboard.

Within Perform, an ordinary view switch preserves a held note on that visible keyboard. Release its owned notes on the final key/pointer up, pointer cancellation, explicit close, roll removal or deactivation, destination change, engine stop, window blur, hidden tab, exit from Perform or successful New/Load/Import. Release to the original channel once, and start a reopened keyboard with released keys. Keyboard cleanup must leave independently playing Manual pads alone. ## Stage 2. Restore Live pad preparation without accidental launches

### 2. Live — identify content and listen before launching

**Interaction.** Each pad shows its number and a persistent content mark that distinguishes a populated pad from an empty one. Keep editing, queued and sounding signals independent of that mark. Give each pad a separately named press-and-hold speaker target. Its accessible name includes device, pad and action. The compact launcher must offer the same preview without requiring a tap on the launch target. If two adjacent targets per pad cannot fit, offer an explicit **Preview** bank mode with a visible return to **Launch**. Keep empty pads selectable, including intentionally silent patterns.

**Implementation.** Compute content from the authored physical pad through the existing melodic, drum, controller, arpeggiator and Score predicates. Preserve their treatment of rests, velocity, curves and hidden notes. Keep the scheduled clip name on arrangement lanes. Reuse the existing speaker gesture with its 250 ms hold, current runtime boundary rules, gesture ownership and release behavior. A short press on the speaker cancels the preview gesture. A press, release or cancelled touch on that target must never fall through to pad selection, a queued launch or a device start. Preserve the current distinctions between a stopped device, a running Manual device and an Arrangement device. ### 3. Live — copy a pad, transpose the copy and build a legacy phrase

**Interaction.** Drag a pad onto another slot of the same device to copy its contents. Provide **Copy pad to…** with the eight numbered destinations for touch and keyboard use. Identify the source and destination before applying a copy to an occupied pad. Give melodic pads up/down transpose controls. A short press moves in scale and a hold applies the existing key-step operation. Name both choices in the pad actions for players using keys or touch. A copy or transpose gesture edits the identified physical pad, even when another pad is playing.

**Implementation.** Share action bindings with the retained device editors. Reuse the melodic, drummer and controller copy callbacks, the Score copy behavior that creates independent note IDs, and the arpeggiator’s existing settings-copy semantics. Preserve each type’s handling of hidden steps, timing, expression, curves, presets and note lengths. Reject self-drops, missing IDs and cross-device payloads. Keep an ordinary launch tap responsive by using a dedicated drag affordance or explicit pad-actions mode. Completing or cancelling a drag must consume its click.

Expose the same physical pad as a source for the legacy pattern workspace. Dropping it on another slot copies content. Dropping it into **Free workspace** (“Drop pads here”) or a definition draft inserts a pad reference at the chosen position. Supply **Insert pad…** inside that workspace so the task also works in single-pane mode. Reuse its existing insertion, hierarchy validation, Group and Apply operations. Preserve the separation between a physical pad, a linked clip draft on the same device and a reusable phrase draft. Only Arrange edits song order.
